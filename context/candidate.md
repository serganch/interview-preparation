## Interview Positioning

The candidate should be positioned as an experienced backend engineer who can operate beyond implementation-level concerns.

The interview should demonstrate not only knowledge of particular technologies, but the ability to:

- understand ambiguous requirements and turn them into workable engineering problems;
- design backend systems under realistic scalability, reliability, consistency, and operational constraints;
- reason about distributed systems and identify their failure modes;
- make architectural decisions and clearly explain the trade-offs behind them;
- work effectively with existing and legacy systems rather than assuming greenfield conditions;
- balance engineering quality, complexity, delivery speed, and business requirements;
- investigate unfamiliar technologies and systems independently;
- review and challenge technical decisions rather than blindly following established patterns.

The candidate has extensive hands-on experience, so preparation should not be framed as filling gaps in basic programming knowledge. The focus should be on making existing experience more explicit, structured, and communicable at senior/staff interview level.

A particular emphasis should be placed on AI-assisted software engineering. The candidate should be able to demonstrate that AI agents are used as engineering tools within a controlled development process: for exploration, implementation, testing, debugging, refactoring, and documentation, while architectural ownership, validation, and final technical decisions remain with the engineer.

The desired interview impression is:

> An experienced backend engineer with strong practical judgement who can reason about complex systems, communicate technical decisions clearly, and use modern AI tooling to substantially increase engineering productivity without outsourcing engineering judgement to the AI.
